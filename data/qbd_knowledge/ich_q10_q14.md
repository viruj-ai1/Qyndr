# ICH Q10 & ICH Q14: Pharmaceutical Quality System & Analytical Procedure Development

## 1. Overview of ICH Q10 & ICH Q14 Integration
Quality by Design (QbD) spans both product/process development and the analytical methodologies used to control and release pharmaceutical products. 
- **ICH Q10: Pharmaceutical Quality System (PQS)** establishes the modern, comprehensive quality management framework across the complete product lifecycle.
- **ICH Q14: Analytical Procedure Development** applies Quality by Design principles to analytical testing methods (Analytical QbD or AQbD), introducing the Analytical Target Profile (ATP) and Method Operable Design Region (MODR).

---

# PART A: ICH Q10 — PHARMACEUTICAL QUALITY SYSTEM (PQS)

## 2. Scope & Objectives across the Product Lifecycle
ICH Q10 describes a model for a pharmaceutical quality system that can be implemented throughout the four distinct lifecycle stages of a product:
1. **Pharmaceutical Development**: Drug substance and drug product design, formulation development, manufacturing process development (ICH Q8), and analytical procedure development (ICH Q14).
2. **Technology Transfer**: Transfer of product knowledge, manufacturing processes, and analytical methods from development to commercial manufacturing or between manufacturing sites.
3. **Commercial Manufacturing**: Acquisition of materials, manufacture and packaging of product, quality control testing, release, storage, and distribution.
4. **Product Discontinuation**: Retention of documentation, sample retention, complaint handling, and continual compliance monitoring.

### 2.1 The Three Primary PQS Objectives
- **Achieve Product Realization**: Establish, adhere to, and maintain a system that consistently delivers products with the quality attributes required to satisfy customer and regulatory requirements.
- **Establish and Maintain a State of Control**: Develop and use effective monitoring and control systems for process performance and product quality to assure continuous process capability.
- **Facilitate Continual Improvement**: Identify and implement improvements to product quality, process capability, variability reduction, and the quality system itself.

---

## 3. The Four Core PQS Elements

### 3.1 Process Performance and Product Quality Monitoring System
- Establishing a proactive monitoring system to track CQAs, CPPs, and in-process variables throughout commercial manufacturing.
- Calculating statistical process capability metrics ($C_p, C_{pk}$, Shewhart control charts) to identify potential drift or shifts before non-conformances occur.
- Serving as the feedback loop to verify that the commercial manufacturing process remains within the ICH Q8 validated Design Space.

### 3.2 Corrective Action and Preventive Action (CAPA) System
- A structured methodology for investigating deviations, complaints, out-of-specification (OOS) results, audits, and non-conformances.
- Determining root causes using structured problem-solving tools (e.g., 5-Whys, Ishikawa, FMEA).
- Implementing corrective actions to remediate identified failures and preventive actions to avoid recurrence across comparable products or processes.

### 3.3 Change Management System
- Formal system ensuring that proposed changes to formulations, raw materials, equipment, process parameters, operating ranges, or analytical methods are scientifically evaluated.
- **Design Space Regulatory Flexibility**: If a process change remains **within the approved Design Space**, the change is managed entirely within the company's internal PQS Change Management System and **does not require regulatory post-approval prior approval or notification**.
- If a proposed change moves outside the approved Design Space or Proven Acceptable Range (PAR), regulatory submission (e.g., Prior Approval Supplement or Variation) is mandatory.

### 3.4 Management Review of Process Performance and Product Quality
- Senior management oversight reviewing the operational performance of processes, audit results, CAPA effectiveness, customer feedback, and regulatory inspection findings.
- Allocating resources for technology upgrades, process modernization, and lifecycle optimization.

---

## 4. The Two Fundamental Enablers of ICH Q10

### 4.1 Knowledge Management
- A systematic approach to acquiring, analyzing, storing, and disseminating information related to products, manufacturing processes, and components.
- Sources include prior scientific knowledge, laboratory DoE studies, scale-up reports, technology transfer reports, and commercial manufacturing historical datasets.

### 4.2 Quality Risk Management (QRM)
- Application of ICH Q9 principles to provide proactive identification and scientific evaluation of quality risks to guide development, validation, and control strategies.

# PART B: ICH Q14 — ANALYTICAL PROCEDURE DEVELOPMENT

## 5. Scope & Analytical Quality by Design (AQbD)
ICH Q14 provides regulatory guidance on the science- and risk-based development of analytical procedures for drug substances and drug products. It introduces the application of Quality by Design to analytical methods (**Analytical QbD / AQbD**).

---

## 6. Analytical Target Profile (ATP)

### 6.1 Regulatory Definition and Concept
The **Analytical Target Profile (ATP)** is a prospective summary of the performance characteristics of an analytical procedure that are necessary to ensure the quality of the measurement, including the required target measurement uncertainty, accuracy, precision, specificity, and reporting range (ICH Q14 Section 3).

### 6.2 Parallel between ATP and QTPP
| Product Development (ICH Q8) | Analytical Development (ICH Q14) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP)**: Defines the required clinical and quality performance of the drug product. | **Analytical Target Profile (ATP)**: Defines what the measurement method must achieve (accuracy, precision, range) without prescribing specific instruments or column chemistry. |
| **Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs)**: E.g., Dissolution rate, impurity levels, assay. | **Analytical Procedure Attributes (APAs)**: Resolution, tailing factor, retention time, signal-to-noise ratio. |
| **Critical Process Parameters (CPPs)**: E.g., Inlet temperature, spray rate, agitation speed. | **Critical Method Parameters (CMPs)**: E.g., Mobile phase pH, column temperature, organic solvent ratio, flow rate. |
| **Design Space (DS)**: Multidimensional operational volume for manufacturing. | **Method Operable Design Region (MODR)**: Multidimensional parameter space for analytical method operation. |

---

## 7. Risk Assessment in Method Development & Critical Method Parameters (CMPs)
- Applying Ishikawa diagrams and FMEA to map analytical variables affecting chromatographic or spectroscopic performance.
- Identifying **Critical Method Parameters (CMPs)** (e.g., gradient slope, buffer concentration, wavelength, sample preparation dilution) whose minor variation leads to failure to satisfy ATP performance criteria.

---

## 8. Method Operable Design Region (MODR) / Analytical Design Space

### 8.1 Definition & Function
The **Method Operable Design Region (MODR)** is the multidimensional combination and interaction of analytical procedure parameters that have been demonstrated to provide assurance of the analytical procedure performance conforming to the ATP.

### 8.2 Operational and Regulatory Advantages
- **Robustness Verification**: Established through multi-factor Design of Experiments (DoE) varying chromatographic parameters simultaneously (e.g., pH $\pm 0.5$, temperature $\pm 5^\circ\text{C}$, organic composition $\pm 3\%$).
- **Post-Approval Change Flexibility**: Adjustments of method parameters within the boundaries of the approved MODR do not require regulatory post-approval variation filings, significantly reducing post-approval regulatory burdens.

---

## 9. Established Conditions (ECs) and Lifecycle Management
- **Established Conditions (ECs)**: Parameter boundaries and commitments that are considered necessary to assure product quality and analytical performance. Changes to ECs require regulatory submission.
- **Supportive Information**: Background developmental data, DoE modeling runs, and exploratory data included in the dossier that are not legally binding ECs.
- **Post-Approval Change Management Protocols (PACMP)**: Pre-agreed regulatory protocols specifying how future analytical changes or transitions between different detection technologies will be validated and reported.
